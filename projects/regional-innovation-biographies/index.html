Overview

Regional Innovation Biographies examines how innovation emerges, circulates, and becomes embedded in less advanced border regions shaped by socio-economic constraints, geopolitical uncertainty, and complex cultural and institutional conditions. Focusing on places such as Wadi Ara and Druze towns in the Golan Heights, the project uses innovation biographies to reconstruct the trajectories of specific innovation processes: where ideas originate, which actors carry them, how knowledge moves between businesses, institutions, customers, suppliers, and external partners, and how regional context affects the capacity to innovate. The project combines qualitative interviews, desk research, egocentric network analysis, geographic analysis, and computational modeling.

It treats regions as relational spaces in which innovation depends not only on proximity, but also on cognitive distance, sectoral similarity, institutional setting, and the structure of local networks. A complementary agent-based/network model simulates how “market signals” and innovation-related information enter a region, move among businesses and organizations, and either diffuse across the regional system or become trapped within closed clusters. By linking innovation biographies with geoinformatic and computational approaches, the project develops a richer way to study regional resilience and development paths.

It shows how different network structures can shape the spread of innovation, suggesting that more distributed and less centralized regional networks may sometimes support broader innovation diffusion than highly efficient networks dominated by a few hubs. The broader aim is to support place-based policy for vulnerable border regions by identifying the social, spatial, and organizational conditions that help local innovation systems adapt, persist, and grow.
