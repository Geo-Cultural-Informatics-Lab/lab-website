Overview

Dynamic Maps in Mobile Apps and the Navigation Experience examines how mobile navigation platforms reshape the relationship between users, space, and economic power. The project focuses on dynamic maps in apps such as Waze, Google Maps, Moovit, Gett, and micromobility platforms, asking how these interfaces do more than represent space: they guide behavior, personalize spatial experience, collect location-based data, and translate movement into economic value. The project develops a critical framework for understanding the “isolated individual” produced through mobile navigation.

In this context, the user moves through physical space while being continuously connected to a platform that interprets the environment, recommends actions, filters what is visible, and mediates relationships with businesses, regulators, transport systems, and other actors. The research asks how dynamic mobile maps reflect and promote economic logics, how they shift agency from users to platforms, and how they may create a growing gap between physical reality and its digital representation. Methodologically, the project combines critical cartography, social semiotics, interface analysis, the walkthrough method, autoethnography, and field-based navigation experiments.

It compares commercial navigation platforms with alternatives such as OpenStreetMap-based tools, records user flows and screenshots, documents physical routes through video and photography, and analyzes how cartographic symbols, interface elements, prompts, icons, advertisements, and route recommendations structure the navigation experience. The broader contribution is both theoretical and methodological. The project offers a way to study mobile map interfaces as powerful visual communication systems, not merely as neutral tools for wayfinding.

It shows how dynamic maps participate in platform capitalism, surveillance, personalization, and the commodification of geographic information, while also developing methods for analyzing the visual and spatial logics through which these processes become part of everyday movement.
