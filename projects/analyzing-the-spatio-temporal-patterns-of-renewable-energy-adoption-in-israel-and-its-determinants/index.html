Analyzing the spatio-temporal patterns of renewable energy adoption in Israel and its determinants

This project analyzes when and where renewable energy technologies are adopted in Israel, using spatial and temporal data to identify social, infrastructural, regulatory, and geographic factors that shape adoption patterns.

Overview

Analyzing the Spatio-Temporal Patterns of Renewable Energy Adoption in Israel and Its Determinants examines how rooftop photovoltaic installations spread across Israeli localities over time, and which local conditions encourage or inhibit adoption. The project focuses on the gap between Israel's high solar-energy potential and the uneven realization of that potential across space, asking why some places adopt rooftop solar rapidly while others lag behind. The study integrates installation records from the Ministry of Energy, national building data, socio-economic data from the Central Bureau of Statistics, Home Front Command alert data, and boundary data from OpenStreetMap.

It analyzes adoption at the locality level, tracking realization rates over time and comparing patterns across settlement types, land-use structures, geographic contexts, and policy environments. Methodologically, the project combines spatial analysis, temporal adoption curves, exploratory visualization, and regression models. It examines factors such as rooftop land-use composition, communal versus non-communal settlement organization, rural and municipal settlement types, proximity to borders, security-related alerts, policy campaigns, approval rates, and spatial-temporal peer effects.

The findings point to strong variation between types of localities, with communal and rural settlements showing distinct adoption trajectories, and with policy campaigns and temporal diffusion effects appearing as important drivers of uptake. The broader aim is to support more realistic renewable-energy planning in Israel: identifying where rooftop solar adoption is likely to accelerate, where intervention may be needed, and how infrastructure planning, tariff design, grid reinforcement, and alternative strategies such as agrivoltaics or ground-mounted installations can respond to actual spatial patterns of adoption.
