A Simulation-Based Urban Dashboard for Improving Municipal Preparedness for Earthquakes

Overview

This project develops an interactive, simulation-based urban dashboard to help municipalities prepare for major earthquakes and evaluate possible response policies before a disaster occurs. The project translates complex agent-based urban simulation models into an accessible online interface where policymakers can select a municipality, define earthquake and policy scenarios, and examine likely medium- and long-term impacts on housing, population, employment, land use, and economic activity. The dashboard is built around municipal “digital twins” that integrate building data, urban assets, socio-demographic information, and synthetic populations generated through spatial allocation methods.

Current case studies include Jerusalem, Be'er Sheva, Tiberias, and Arad. The model incorporates earthquake damage estimates for reference scenarios such as Naot HaKikar, Beit She'an, and Yagur, including expected impacts on buildings and displaced residents. A central aim of the project is to let users test policy interventions, including land-use stabilization, temporary shelter for displaced residents, and economic support for households or businesses.

By comparing baseline and intervention scenarios, the dashboard can reveal how different recovery strategies may affect urban trajectories over time.
