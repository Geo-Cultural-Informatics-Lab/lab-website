Overview

The Impact of Corporate Editors on Collective Intelligence in OpenStreetMap examines how the growing involvement of corporate mapping teams changes the social and informational dynamics of OpenStreetMap. The project focuses on the tension between OSM as a volunteer-driven, open geographic information project and the increasing participation of companies such as Meta/Facebook, Microsoft, Apple, Uber, and Lyft, which contribute to OSM while also relying on it for their own commercial needs. The project asks whether corporate editing strengthens or weakens OSM's collective intelligence.

On one hand, corporate contributors may improve coverage, completeness, and data quality at scale. On the other hand, their activity may affect volunteer motivation, alter contribution practices, concentrate influence, or change the balance between community priorities and corporate needs. Methodologically, the project combines large-scale analysis of OSM historical data with qualitative research.

It uses OSM editing histories, including tools such as OSHDB, to compare corporate and volunteer contribution patterns across selected countries and regions where corporate mapping teams are active. It then links these quantitative patterns with interviews and surveys of highly active volunteers, less active contributors, and corporate mappers, in order to understand how different actors perceive and respond to corporate involvement. The broader contribution is to understand what happens when commercial actors enter open, volunteer-based knowledge infrastructures.

By studying changes in data quality, contribution diversity, mapper behavior, and community response, the project offers a framework for assessing when corporate participation supports collective intelligence and when it may reshape, narrow, or undermine it.
