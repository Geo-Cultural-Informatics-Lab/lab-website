Overview

Gaps in Accessibility to Public Land Uses in Mixed Cities in Israel examines whether Jewish and Arab residents in mixed Israeli cities have equal and equitable access to public urban resources. The project focuses on public land uses such as schools, houses of worship, community centers, libraries, playgrounds, elderly day centers, and parks, asking how their spatial distribution reflects broader questions of urban justice, equity, and the right to the city. The study combines GIS-based accessibility analysis with a planning-history perspective.

It measures walking-time access from residential buildings to public land uses through the street network, accounting for distance, topography, a 15-minute walking threshold, population size, and, for parks, area per resident. Accessibility results are then compared with demographic and socio-economic characteristics, including the share of Arab residents, socio-economic status, and education levels. Methodologically, the project uses spatial analysis, inequality measures inspired by the Gini index, Pearson correlations, and weighted assessments of whether unequal access works for or against groups with greater need.

A second qualitative stage uses interviews and textual analysis of plans, policy documents, and municipal or planning committee records to understand how historical and planning decisions produced current accessibility gaps. The broader contribution is to move from general claims about inequality in mixed cities to a spatially grounded, empirically testable account of urban equity. By linking public land-use distribution with ethnicity, class, planning history, and everyday access, the project helps assess whether mixed cities provide public resources in ways that support shared urban citizenship or reproduce unequal geographies of service provision.
