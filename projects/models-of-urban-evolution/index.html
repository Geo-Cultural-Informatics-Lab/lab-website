Overview

Models of Urban Evolution is a University of Toronto-Hebrew University research and training alliance that studies how cities, communities, and urban forms grow, change, and travel across contexts. The project connects the Urban Genome model of urban evolution, including the idea that cities can be analyzed through evolving urban forms and cultural-spatial codes, with HUJI research on cities as complex adaptive assemblages, informal urban development, placemaking, and geo-cultural informatics. It brings together comparative cases from Israel, Canada, and other settings, including Bedouin settlements, Palestinian towns, East Jerusalem, Toronto public spaces, cemeteries, threshold spaces, and informal settlements.
