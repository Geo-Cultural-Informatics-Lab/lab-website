Geo-cultural narrative analysis of the built environment

Overview

This project develops geo-cultural informatic methods for reconstructing cultural histories from the spatial organization of built environments. It starts from the idea that settlements, cemeteries, public institutions, religious sites, streets, plots, and architectural forms are not only physical objects, but also spatial traces of social norms, cultural values, collective identities, and historical change. A central case study focuses on Jewish communities in Central Moravia before World War II.

Many of these communities ceased to exist during the Holocaust, leaving only partial documentary, material, and spatial evidence. The project uses the organization of Jewish settlements and cemeteries to recover aspects of their cultural history: where key communal functions were located, how cemeteries developed over time, who was buried where and next to whom, how grave designs and inscriptions changed, and how spatial patterns moved between communities across the region. Methodologically, the project combines qualitative interpretation with geoinformatic and spatial analysis.

It codes graves, plots, settlement elements, inscriptions, identities, family relations, design features, and spatial relations, then analyzes their temporal and geographic patterns. The aim is to identify spatial typologies, reconstruct local cultural timelines, and trace broader regional processes such as migration, integration, religious change, and the diffusion of social norms. The broader contribution is methodological as well as historical: it treats the built environment as a cultural archive and develops transferable tools for deriving historical-cultural narratives from spatial data, especially where written records are incomplete or communities have disappeared.
